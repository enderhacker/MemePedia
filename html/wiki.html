All Your Base Are Belong To Us

From MemePedia, the free encyclopedia.
All Your Base
The villain "CATS" from Zero Wing.
Origin: Zero Wing (Sega Genesis)
Year: 2001
Type: Flash Animation / Catchphrase

"All your base are belong to us" (often abbreviated as AYBABTU) is a broken English phrase that became an internet phenomenon in 2001. The quote comes from the opening cutscene of the 1991 European Sega Mega Drive version of the video game Zero Wing.

[edit]Origins

The meme took off when a Flash animation by Bad_CRC was posted to Newgrounds in early 2001. It featured a techno remix of the game's soundtrack (the "Invasion of the Gabber Robots" remix by The Laziest Men on Mars).

[edit]Transcript

Captain: What happen ?
Mechanic: Somebody set up us the bomb.
Operator: We get signal.
Captain: What !
Operator: Main screen turn on.
Captain: It's you !!
CATS: How are you gentlemen !! All your base are belong to us.

Star Wars Kid

From MemePedia, the free encyclopedia.
Star Wars Kid
The original footage.
Year: 2002 (Filmed), 2003 (Viral)
Type: Viral Video

Star Wars Kid refers to a viral video showing a high school student, Ghyslain Raza, wielding a golf ball retriever as if it were a double-sided lightsaber from the movie Star Wars Episode I: The Phantom Menace.

[edit]Spread

The video was filmed in November 2002 but was left in a school basement. It was discovered by other students in 2003 and uploaded to the file-sharing network Kazaa. Within weeks, it had been downloaded millions of times.

It is widely considered one of the first "viral videos" in internet history, pre-dating YouTube. Users created "remixes" adding special effects (lightsaber glows) and music.

Numa Numa

From MemePedia, the free encyclopedia.
Numa Numa Dance
Gary Brolsma mid-dance.
Origin: Newgrounds
Year: 2004
Song: Dragostea Din Tei

Numa Numa is an internet phenomenon based on a video of American vlogger Gary Brolsma lip-syncing to the Moldovan pop song "Dragostea Din Tei" by O-Zone. The title comes from the misheard refrain "nu mă, nu mă iei".

[edit]Impact

Uploaded to Newgrounds.com on December 6, 2004, under the title Numa Numa Dance, it instantly became a hit due to Brolsma's energetic and unselfconscious performance. It has been parodied in South Park and by thousands of imitators on early YouTube.

Rickrolling

From MemePedia, the free encyclopedia.
Rickrolling
Never Gonna Give You Up (1987)
Origin: 4chan (/v/)
Year: 2007
Type: Bait-and-Switch

Rickrolling is a bait-and-switch prank that involves posting a hyperlink that is supposedly relevant to the topic at hand, but instead leads to the music video for Rick Astley's 1987 hit "Never Gonna Give You Up".

[edit]History

The trend evolved from an earlier 4chan prank called "duckrolling" (linking to a picture of a duck on wheels). The first known instance of Rickrolling occurred in May 2007 on the /v/ (video games) board, where a user claimed to post a link to the Grand Theft Auto IV trailer.

[edit]Mainstream Success

On April 1, 2008, YouTube pranked the entire internet by redirecting all featured homepage videos to the Rickroll. This event cemented the meme's place in history.

Keyboard Cat

From MemePedia, the free encyclopedia.
Fatso the Cat
"Play him off, Keyboard Cat!"
Year: 1984 (Filmed), 2007/2009 (Viral)
Origin: YouTube

Keyboard Cat is a video of a female cat named Fatso wearing a blue shirt and "playing" an electronic keyboard. The footage was actually filmed by Charlie Schmidt in 1984 on VHS but was not uploaded to YouTube until 2007.

[edit]"Play Him Off"

The meme achieved peak popularity in 2009 when Brad O'Farrell created a mashup video. The format involves showing a "fail" video (someone tripping or making a mistake), followed immediately by Keyboard Cat playing his upbeat tune to "play them off stage."
